In 1932, Australia declared war on a bird. And lost.
After World War One, veterans were given land to farm wheat in Western Australia. Then 20,000 emus showed up. They ate the crops. They trampled the fences. So the farmers asked the army for help.
The army sent Major Meredith. Two soldiers. Two machine guns. And 10,000 bullets.
Day one: the emus simply ran out of range. Day three: a thousand emus walked up to a water dam. Perfect ambush. The gun fired, got twelve birds, and jammed.
So Meredith got creative. He strapped a machine gun to a truck. But the emus were faster than the truck. And the ground was so bumpy, the gunner couldn't fire a single shot.
After six days, the army pulled out. Then came back. By December, they had fired 9,860 bullets. Ten bullets for every emu.
Meredith said: if we had a division with the bullet-carrying capacity of these birds, it would face any army in the world.
The emus are still there. The army is not.
